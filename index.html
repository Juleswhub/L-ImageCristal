Cruise Me, Cruise Me Babe

In Distribution
During a late-night phone conversation with a mysterious girl named Bliss, whose only visible feature is her lips, an insomniac photographer recalls witnessing a surreal encounter: the soul of a man in a tuxedo floating in the sky, guided through the streets of Luxembourg by an angel. Her search leads her to a church and a hidden bar populated by veiled figures, where the boundaries between memory, dream, grief, and reality begin to dissolve.


Duration: 16 min
Production: Colgado Productions • L'image Cristal
Supported by Film Fund Luxembourg, Schëtter Commune, Bernard-Massard
Genre: Surreal drama. Intended to give life to Belgian painter René Magritte's surreal creations, inspired the poetic tension of David Lynch.

About L'Image Cristal

Unbound by restrictive editorial dogma, we strive in the in-between: between auteur and genre, between fiction’s inventions and formal, aesthetic experience shaking stories. We welcome stories that dare lyrically, poetically, strangely, fantastically, and forms that break their own walls. This is why we do not restrict ourselves to pure cinema but explore other visual storytelling, such as photography or installation art. L'Image Cristal was born from co-writers and directors, Jules Wax and Alexis Vandeweerd, as a need to unravel visually challenging stories that beautifully blend fiction with experimental components, as a way to appeal to the senses and embark its audience on an audiovisual experience.


Their work has been featured at international film festivals, including LuxFilmFest, FilmFestival Max Ophuls Preis, and the INDIE SHORT AWARDS Buenos Aires. They've also contributed to cultural exhibitions in Luxembourg, including Galerie 39, Dudelange, University of Luxembourg, or the Annual International IAWIS Congress on Water and Sea through the lens of the humanities.


They approach each project with curiosity and attention to detail, whether directing short films, designing user experiences, or creating immersive installations. Their goal is to create work that resonates visually and emotionally, leaning towards formal experimentation rather than being mentally challenging.
